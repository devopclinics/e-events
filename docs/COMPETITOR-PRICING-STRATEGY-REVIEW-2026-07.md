# Festio Competitor Review and Pricing Strategy (Team Draft)

Date: 2026-07-24
Purpose: Internal working draft for pricing-model finalization.
Scope: Direct competitors, transaction-first alternatives, and practical substitution threats.

## 1) Executive Summary
Festio competes in two buying motions at once:
- Relationship-led curated events (Gatsby-style motion)
- Operations + messaging + ticketing execution (broader event ops motion)

This creates a pricing strategy requirement:
- Be premium enough to win serious teams
- Remain transparent enough to beat opaque quote-only competitors
- Keep unit economics healthy on messaging-heavy events

Recommended pricing posture:
1. Keep at least one visible public pricing path.
2. Introduce a stronger premium lane (concierge + integrations + governance).
3. Make messaging economics explicit (credit usage, reserve/refund logic, overage bands).
4. Separate enterprise trust/integration value from pure attendee volume pricing.

- RSVPify presents multiple pricing/table representations across sections and variants; treat the exact numeric values as review-time snapshots and re-check before publishing externally.

## 4) Detailed Competitive Playbook

## 4.1 Gatsby Events
Buyer profile:
- VC/PE, executive offices, relationship-driven invite-only programs.

Strengths:
- Tight narrative, premium brand posture, trust framing.
- CRM-forward language and security signaling.

Weaknesses:
- Opaque pricing discovery.
- Less obvious fit for transactional ticketing and broad operational workflows.

Festio win strategy:
- Position: "Curation + full execution depth".
- Product proof: ticketing, messaging channels, operations modules, cross-team workflows.
- Pricing move: keep public baseline + premium lane; avoid black-box quoting for SMB/mid-market.
- Sales talk track: transparent TCO with enterprise path when needed.

## 4.2 RSVPify
Buyer profile:
- Premium private events, nonprofits, organizations wanting polished RSVP flow.

Strengths:
- Mature RSVP and event registration positioning.

Weaknesses:
- Can feel registration-centric versus full operational stack.

Festio win strategy:
- Lead with "beyond registration": access control, day-of operations, guest communication continuity.
- Price against them on breadth-per-dollar, not just feature parity.

## 4.3 EventCreate
Buyer profile:
- Teams valuing design flexibility and broad event setup features.

Strengths:
- Marketing/page-building friendliness.

Weaknesses:
- Add-on complexity can raise effective cost.

Festio win strategy:
- Show integrated operations and clearer package boundaries.
- Highlight fewer handoffs between registration and venue operations.

## 4.4 Cvent
Buyer profile:
- Large enterprises with procurement/compliance requirements.

Strengths:
- Enterprise footprint and procurement familiarity.

Weaknesses:
- Cost/complexity for smaller teams; longer onboarding.

Festio win strategy:
- Compete where speed and operational usability matter.
- Offer enterprise add-ons without enterprise-level implementation friction.

## 4.5 Bizzabo
Buyer profile:
- B2B marketing organizations and conference-heavy teams.

Strengths:
- Event marketing ecosystem credibility.

Weaknesses:
- Can be overkill for relationship-led private event programs.

Festio win strategy:
- Sell simpler control model and faster event operator adoption.
- Anchor on relationship events + day-of reliability.

## 4.6 Splash
Buyer profile:
- Event marketing and brand teams.

Strengths:
- Brand and campaign orientation.

Weaknesses:
- Less compelling if buyer needs deep operational execution.

Festio win strategy:
- Emphasize operations + messaging + access control in one stack.

## 4.7 Swoogo
Buyer profile:
- Structured registration and conference workflows.

Strengths:
- Reliable registration and event structure.

Weaknesses:
- Not always strongest in multi-channel guest operations.

Festio win strategy:
- Demonstrate communication and day-of operations depth tied to guest records.

## 4.8 Eventbrite
Buyer profile:
- Paid public events and fast checkout needs.

Strengths:
- Category familiarity and straightforward launch.

Weaknesses:
- Transaction-fee economics can get expensive at scale.

Festio win strategy:
- Compare total economics at target volume.
- Differentiate on curated guest workflows and private-event sophistication.

## 4.9 TicketTailor
Buyer profile:
- Cost-conscious paid event operators.

Strengths:
- Low-fee and pricing transparency story.

Weaknesses:
- Less complete relationship and operations narrative.

Festio win strategy:
- Maintain transparent pricing and prove additional operational value.

## 4.10 Tito / Humanitix
Buyer profile:
- Lean teams running straightforward paid registrations.

Strengths:
- Simplicity and price clarity.

Weaknesses:
- Limited depth for complex relationship operations.

Festio win strategy:
- Offer growth path: start simple, unlock advanced operations without migration.

## 5) Pricing Model Recommendations for Festio

### 5.1 Architecture
Keep a three-lane model:
1. Self-serve lane:
- Clear public tiers.
- Predictable attendee and message allowances.

2. Growth lane:
- Adds advanced operations (access zones, deeper messaging controls, premium templates, operations modules).
- Transparent overage mechanics.

3. Enterprise lane:
- Security/compliance support, integration priority, account governance, implementation support.
- Custom quote, but anchored by published reference ranges.

### 5.2 Core Principles
- Principle 1: No hidden economic traps.
- Principle 2: Publish what is included vs add-on clearly.
- Principle 3: Keep messaging economics explicit and auditable.
- Principle 4: Charge premiums for trust and integration outcomes, not just arbitrary feature walls.

### 5.3 Messaging Credit Policy (important)
- Define billable event clearly (submitted vs delivered).
- Reserve/refund logic must be explicit in UI and docs.
- Overage bands should be visible before purchase.
- Show expected spend calculator by event size and channel mix.

### 5.4 Suggested Packaging Skeleton
- Free / Trial:
- RSVP, basic email invite, limited guests.

- Starter:
- Branded RSVP, basic check-in, starter messaging credits.

- Professional:
- Seating/access workflows, stronger communication tooling, larger credits.

- Scale:
- Multi-event ops features, priority support, analytics depth.

- Enterprise:
- Governance, integration acceleration, security assurances, tailored onboarding.

## 6) Win/Loss Guidance for Sales and Product

### 6.1 Where Festio should aggressively compete
- Relationship-driven teams that also need operational depth.
- Buyers frustrated with opaque pricing.
- Organizations needing channel flexibility (email + messaging pathways).

### 6.2 Where Festio should qualify hard
- Accounts requiring immediate enterprise proofs that are not yet finalized.
- Buyers choosing purely on brand prestige over operational capability.

### 6.3 Deal desk rules (pricing discipline)
- Never discount without usage assumptions documented.
- For strategic discounts, trade for:
- annual commitment,
- testimonial/case study rights,
- defined usage floors.

## 7) 90-Day Pricing Validation Plan
1. Run 3 pricing experiments:
- Experiment A: visible tier comparison redesign.
- Experiment B: messaging overage presentation (flat vs tiered).
- Experiment C: enterprise lane anchor wording and CTA.

2. Track these KPIs weekly:
- Pricing-page conversion to trial/demo.
- Sales-qualified lead rate by segment.
- Average projected gross margin per event.
- Messaging gross margin by channel mix.
- Win rate vs top 3 named competitors.

3. Decision gate at day 90:
- Keep, adjust, or rebuild model based on margin + win-rate outcomes.

## 8) Team Review Checklist (Use in Final Pricing Meeting)
- Do we want to remain publicly priced for core tiers? (recommended: yes)
- Which capabilities are true premium differentiators vs expected baseline?
- Are messaging economics understandable in under 60 seconds?
- Can sales explain why Festio wins against Gatsby in one sentence?
- Are discount guardrails documented and enforced?
- Do we have a clear upgrade path from Starter -> Professional -> Scale -> Enterprise?

## 9) Final Recommendation
Recommended final direction:
- Keep transparent public pricing for core plans.
- Introduce an explicit premium/enterprise lane for trust, governance, and integrations.
- Make messaging pricing auditable and customer-trust friendly.
- Sell Festio as "relationship-event quality plus full event operations execution," not as just another RSVP tool.
